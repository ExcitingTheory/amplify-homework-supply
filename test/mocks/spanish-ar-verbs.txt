Introduction to Spanish Verbs

Regular -AR Verbs in Present Tense

Spanish verbs are categorized by their infinitive endings: -ar, -er, or -ir. This lesson focuses on regular -ar verbs in the present tense.

Essential Vocabulary

hablar - to speak, to talk
The most common -ar verb. Used for any type of speaking or talking.
Example: Yo hablo español. (I speak Spanish.)

trabajar - to work
Refers to employment or any type of work activity.
Example: Ella trabaja en una oficina. (She works in an office.)

estudiar - to study
Used for academic studying or learning.
Example: Nosotros estudiamos matemáticas. (We study mathematics.)

bailar - to dance
Any form of dancing.
Example: Ellos bailan salsa. (They dance salsa.)

cantar - to sing
The act of singing.
Example: Tú cantas bien. (You sing well.)

caminar - to walk
To walk or move on foot.
Example: Él camina al trabajo. (He walks to work.)

cocinar - to cook
To prepare food.
Example: Mi madre cocina la cena. (My mother cooks dinner.)

nadar - to swim
The action of swimming.
Example: Los niños nadan en la piscina. (The children swim in the pool.)

escuchar - to listen (to)
To listen or pay attention to sounds.
Example: Escucho música. (I listen to music.)

mirar - to watch, to look at
To watch or observe something.
Example: Miramos la televisión. (We watch television.)

comprar - to buy
To purchase something.
Example: Compro pan en la panadería. (I buy bread at the bakery.)

viajar - to travel
To journey or take trips.
Example: Viajan a México. (They travel to Mexico.)

descansar - to rest
To relax or take a break.
Example: Descansas después del trabajo. (You rest after work.)

llevar - to carry, to wear
Can mean to carry something or to wear clothing.
Example: Llevo una camisa azul. (I'm wearing a blue shirt.)

tomar - to take, to drink
Has multiple meanings: to take, to drink, to grab.
Example: Tomo café por la mañana. (I drink coffee in the morning.)

Conjugation Pattern

For regular -ar verbs, remove the -ar ending and add these endings:

yo - o (I)
tú - as (you, informal)
él/ella/usted - a (he/she/you formal)
nosotros/nosotras - amos (we)
vosotros/vosotras - áis (you all, Spain)
ellos/ellas/ustedes - an (they/you all)

Example: HABLAR (to speak)
yo hablo
tú hablas
él/ella/usted habla
nosotros/nosotras hablamos
vosotros/vosotras habláis
ellos/ellas/ustedes hablan

Additional Important Verbs

buscar - to look for, to search
Example: Busco mis llaves. (I'm looking for my keys.)

enseñar - to teach, to show
Example: El profesor enseña español. (The teacher teaches Spanish.)

esperar - to wait, to hope
Example: Esperamos el autobús. (We wait for the bus.)

ganar - to win, to earn
Example: Ganan dinero. (They earn money.)

llegar - to arrive
Example: Llego a las ocho. (I arrive at eight.)

necesitar - to need
Example: Necesitas estudiar. (You need to study.)

pagar - to pay
Example: Pago la cuenta. (I pay the bill.)

pasar - to pass, to spend (time), to happen
Example: Pasamos el verano en la playa. (We spend summer at the beach.)

preguntar - to ask (a question)
Example: Pregunto la hora. (I ask the time.)

preparar - to prepare
Example: Preparan la comida. (They prepare the food.)

Practice Sentences

1. Los estudiantes estudian en la biblioteca. (The students study in the library.)
2. Yo hablo con mis amigos todos los días. (I talk with my friends every day.)
3. Ella trabaja en un hospital. (She works in a hospital.)
4. Nosotros compramos frutas en el mercado. (We buy fruits at the market.)
5. ¿Tú cantas en la ducha? (Do you sing in the shower?)
6. Ellos viajan a España cada año. (They travel to Spain every year.)
7. Yo cocino pasta para la cena. (I cook pasta for dinner.)
8. Ustedes caminan al parque. (You all walk to the park.)

Time Expressions Often Used with Present Tense

todos los días - every day
siempre - always
nunca - never
a veces - sometimes
generalmente - generally
frecuentemente - frequently
por la mañana - in the morning
por la tarde - in the afternoon
por la noche - in the evening/at night

Common Mistakes to Avoid

1. Don't forget to conjugate! Each subject needs its own verb ending.
2. Remember that "yo" form ends in -o, not -ar.
3. Watch out for spelling changes in verbs like "buscar" and "llegar" in certain tenses (though not present).
4. Don't confuse "hablar" (to speak) with "escuchar" (to listen).

Summary

Regular -ar verbs follow a predictable pattern in Spanish. Once you learn this pattern, you can conjugate hundreds of verbs. Practice is essential - try using these verbs in your own sentences about daily activities. As you progress, you'll learn that some -ar verbs have stem changes or spelling changes in certain tenses, but the basic pattern remains consistent.
